Doors

Privacy

Doors has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no cookies. Your plans live on your device. To rank doors, route your days and answer your questions, some of what you type goes to the services below, and this page says exactly what.

Applies to the web app at this address and the Doors iPhone app. Last updated October 24, 2026.

The short version

Your profile, plans, visit notes and contacts are stored on your device only. Doors' server keeps no copy of them. Before any AI model reads your chat or a visit note, the server replaces the names it knows about (yours and your contacts') with "the contact". The server never stores your IP address: it keeps a one-way hash of it, only to count each day's plan builds per visitor (details below).

What stays on your device

  • Web: your brand profile, each saved plan with its door statuses, visit outcomes, notes and contacts, the doors you saved for later, your activity in My doors (what you did at each door, with times), your rules, and copies of plans Doors built for you are kept in your browser's local storage. The chat with Doors and the guided demo's progress are kept in session storage, which clears when you close the tab. The page's own files are cached for offline use; your data is not in that cache.
  • iPhone app: the same things, in the app's own storage on the phone, plus a copy of Doors' public city files so a saved plan opens with no signal.
  • Deleting a plan in Doors, clearing the site's data in your browser, or deleting the app removes it. There is no server copy to delete.

What goes to Doors' server, and what it keeps

Doors runs on Cloudflare Workers. Your device talks to Doors' server, never directly to Qloo, Anthropic, Google or OpenRouteService; the API keys stay on the server.

  • Building a plan for a brand or city Doors doesn't already hold sends the brand names and the city you typed. The result (ranked doors for that brand's drinkers in that city, from Qloo) is kept for 30 days under the brand and city, so the next person who asks for the same pair gets it without another Qloo call. Plans built from the public brand-and-city pairs are listed as saved runs others can open. Plans built from "doors that already pour you" are not listed. None of these results contains your profile, name, notes or contacts.
  • Asking Doors, or telling it how a visit went, sends your message (up to 600 characters; a visit note up to 400) and a summary of the plan on screen: the city, your brand and product, your price per case and your ask (with the price math Doors works out from them), the plan's days and hours, each door's name, rank, status, outcome and notes, the current door's talking points, and your rules. The names Doors knows about are also sent, only so the server can remove them before a model reads the rest (below). Nothing from a chat or a visit is stored on the server.
  • Your IP address: to stop one visitor using up the shared Qloo budget, the server counts live runs per visitor per day. The count is kept under a one-way hash of your IP address (for IPv6, of the first half of it: your network, not your device), salted with a secret and with the date, so it can't be turned back into your address or matched across days. The first plan built on a new day replaces the day's list of hashes; if no one builds one, the whole counter expires after 70 days. Searches and chat turns are also rate-limited per address, in memory and by Cloudflare at its edge; those counts are not stored by Doors.
  • Road answers: drive times and distances between coordinates are cached for 30 days (coordinates only). Address lookups are not kept.
  • Taking a plan to your home screen (web): Safari gives a home-screen app its own storage, so the page puts your plan (profile, statuses, outcomes, contacts, rules) into the address of its app manifest. The server reads that address to answer and does not store it.
  • The server logs nothing about you. It logs a model's error message when a model fails, which never includes what you sent, and, when it builds a trade brief, that brief's category, city and cost.

Who else sees what

  • Qloo (taste data): the brand or door names and the city you typed, and Qloo's own ids for venues and brands. Never your name, notes, contacts or IP address. Searches send what you type in the search box (up to 80 characters), with the city's name for a place search.
  • Anthropic (Claude Sonnet, claude-sonnet-5-5) answers Doors Agent. It is sent your chat messages and visit notes with the plan summary above, after the server replaces the names it knows (yours as you sign your notes, your saved contacts') and names it finds beside a role or a verb ("talked to Sam", "the manager is Priya") or before a personal detail ("Jo is 60") with "the contact", replaces phone numbers and email addresses with "a phone number" and "an email address", and leaves out what a note says about a person (age, gender, origin, religion, sexuality, health, family, body or politics). The names are put back on your screen when the answer arrives. These are pattern checks: a name or a detail they don't recognise is sent as written, so leave out what you wouldn't want a third party to read. When a question needs the web (a door's news, menu or events), Anthropic's web search is sent search words the model writes; it is asked to use only the door's name and the city, which are public, and nothing else checks that; the pages it cites are listed under the answer with their links. Doors also asks Anthropic, with web search, for a short trade brief on a drink category in a city (the category and the city only, nothing of yours) and keeps it on Cloudflare for everyone who plans that category there. Anthropic handles these requests under its own commercial terms. When you tap for polished Day done notes, the day's logged facts (doors, outcomes, your notes) go to Anthropic once, names removed the same way. A model (Anthropic's Claude Sonnet, claude-sonnet-5-5) writes each door's talking points (the opener, the angle, the line for the buyer, the questions, the points, the pushback and the drafted message to the door) and Doors Agent's replies from Qloo's data about that door; the door pages don't say so, this page does. The written opener and message carry a blank where your name goes; your device fills it in, so your name is not sent. For the talking points it is sent Qloo's facts about the door, your brand, product and ask, and who you said drinks it (never your name), and Doors keeps the result on Cloudflare for anyone with the same door, product and ask.
  • When Anthropic can't answer, Cloudflare Workers AI (Meta's Llama) and then Google Gemini are sent the same thing, names removed the same way, on their free tiers and under their own terms; on its free tier, Google may use what it is sent to improve its products. Doors' own rules answer when no model is available. When Doors builds a plan for a brand and city it doesn't hold yet, Gemini also drafts the short lines about each route door (its crowd, the door and an opener) from Qloo's description of that door; nothing of yours is in that request. The crowd and room lines, and the first sentence of many openers, on the doors Doors already holds were drafted the same way, before publishing, by Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite (gemini-3.1-flash-lite); the door pages say written or drafted by a model and leave the model's name here.
  • OpenRouteService (roads and addresses): coordinates for drive times; the start address you type, as you type it (from 3 characters), with the city's centre to the nearest kilometre, to find it on the map.
  • Map tiles: your browser loads map images from OpenFreeMap (falling back to Doors' own copy, then OpenStreetMap). The iPhone app shows Apple Maps. Like any website, these services see your IP address and which part of the map you look at.
  • Venue photos are loaded from Qloo's image server, which sees your IP address.
  • Navigate opens Google Maps, Apple Maps or Waze with the door's location, only when you tap it; Google Maps and Apple Maps are also sent where you drive from (the previous door, or your start). When Qloo has no website for a door, its link opens a DuckDuckGo search for the door's name and city.
  • Voice goes to Cloudflare Workers AI. Tapping the microphone records what you say until you stop talking and sends that short clip to OpenAI's Whisper model on Workers AI to turn it into words (on the web, drive mode first tries your browser's own speech recognition; Chrome sends that audio to Google). Spoken answers and read-alouds come from Deepgram's Aura-2 voice on Workers AI, sent the words on screen; when it can't answer, your device's own voice reads them (the guided tour uses only your device's voice). Neither the clip nor the words are stored or logged by Doors, and the iPhone app deletes the clip as soon as it is sent. On your first visit note on the web, Doors may record beside the browser's own recognition, so the clip is ready if the browser hears nothing; it is sent only then and dropped otherwise.
  • Cloudflare hosts Doors and handles all of its traffic under its own privacy policy.

What Doors doesn't do

  • No accounts or sign-in, no cookies, no analytics, ads or tracking scripts. Fonts and the map library are served by Doors itself, not a third-party CDN.
  • No selling or sharing of your data, and no use of it beyond answering the request you made.
  • Doors is for people at work planning sales visits. It is not meant for children.
